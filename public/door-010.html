Geometry

Polygon. Forward lunge wedge with vertical arm column (front bent knee, back straight leg, arms overhead).

One foot forward, one angled back and grounded; front knee tracks over the ankle without collapsing inward; torso lifts and arms reach up. Prefer feet on parallel tracks (hip-width) over a tightrope line. Turn the chest forward within comfort; do not wrench the back knee by forcing the pelvis fully square.

Adiyogi

VBT dharana 010

School class form: in Warrior I, let soft gaze rest in the open field above and between the hands (empty sky), keep attention there without chasing thoughts, and meet the still quality of that field like a quiet wall. Optional contemplative variant off-mat: briefly rest attention on a worthy presence you admire, then return to breath and stance. Practice is contemplative focus, not ritual or force.

Safety

  1. Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  2. If the flat back foot or hip rotation agitates the back knee or hip, use High Crescent Lunge (back heel lifted, hips freer) or shorten the stance. Do not force the pelvis square.
  3. General practice caution only for knees, hips, low back, and neck (especially if looking up). Not medical advice; regress depth or exit when needed.

Five ways

  1. Door D-010: Warrior I, Virabhadrasana I. A standing forward lunge with arms reaching up.
  2. Step one foot forward, angle the back foot and plant the heel. Bend the front knee over the ankle. Reach the arms up. Keep the feet hip-width for balance.
  3. Face the chest forward within comfort. Root the outer edge of the back foot. Lift the crown. Soft gaze forward—or to the thumbs only if the neck is free.
  4. Breathe equal fours: inhale lengthen the reach; exhale root the back heel. Six easy cycles. No strain.
  5. Lower the arms. Straighten the front leg. Step back to Mountain. If anything felt sharp, choose Crescent or a shorter stance next time. Pain means pause.
