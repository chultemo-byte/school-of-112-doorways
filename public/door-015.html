Look here

First stage: look forward with a concave back. Full fold: soft gaze toward the floor or eyes softly closed.

Water

Inhale lengthen the spine long; exhale hinge and settle without dumping weight on the head. Rest one beat in the quiet after sound fades.

Geometry

Polygon. Wide-base inverted V (A-frame): two long leg lines from a broad foot base to the hip hinge, then a lengthened spine vector toward the floor; optional crown contact as a soft third point.

Feet stand wide and parallel (or toes slightly in), legs firm, pelvis hinges forward. Hands land under the shoulders first with a long concave spine; then elbows bend and the crown may meet floor or a block while weight stays in the legs, not the head.

Safety

  1. Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  2. Keep hands on blocks or a chair with a concave spine; skip crown-to-floor. Soften knees if hamstrings or low back protest.
  3. General practice caution only; not medical advice. Use support under hands or head; avoid dumping weight onto the neck. If dizziness appears in the fold, come up slowly with hands on hips.

Five ways

  1. Door D-015: Wide-Legged Standing Forward Fold — Prasarita Padottanasana. A wide stance that folds the spine long toward quiet.
  2. Step the feet wide and parallel. Firm the legs. Hinge at the hips. Hands under the shoulders first; spine long. Crown may meet floor or a block later—legs still carry you.
  3. Gravity lives in the feet. Keep the head light. Look forward while the back stays concave; only then soften the gaze down as you fold.
  4. Inhale four to lengthen. Exhale six to hinge and settle. Six cycles. Land in the still gap after the exhale.
  5. Inhale, lift the head, hands to hips, rise with a long spine. Feet walk in. Stand quiet for one breath and notice the same quiet without the fold.
