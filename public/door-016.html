Door 016 · House 1 · Mūlādhāra

Praṇava Fading into Void

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 16 · verse 39 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 16Verse 39sound · mantra · emptiness/spacePracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

Sound a soft, long Oṃ, aloud or silently, and rest in the quiet gap when the sound ends.

The verse

praṇavādisamuccārāt plutānte śūnyabhāvanāt | śūnyayā parayā śaktyā śūnyatām eti bhairavi ||

O Bhairavī, by fully sounding the praṇava (such as Oṃ) and contemplating emptiness at the end of its prolonged (pluta) sound, one reaches emptiness through the supreme power that is emptiness.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer; not a quotation.

Commentary

Lakshmanjoo (via the Lakshmanjoo Academy) names three praṇavas, Oṃ, Hūṃ and Hrīṃ, sounded long with attention resting where the sound dissolves. Singh's note gives the same three (Vedic Auṃ, Śaiva Hūṃ, Śākta Hrīṃ) and stresses that at the end of the protracted sound one contemplates the void, not the usual ardhacandra, bindu and other subtle phases.

How to sit with it

Sound a soft, drawn-out Oṃ (or another seed-syllable you have been taught), or sound it silently. At the moment the sound ends, rest in the quiet gap. Do not chase the next thought. Do not force breath retention or strain to lengthen the sound.

Where attention rests

The moment the sound ends, and the silence after it

Not reaching for the next thought or the next sound

Not a body shape. The map is one long sound tapering to nothing, and the open silence after it.

Breath

Inhale easily. Let the out-breath carry a long, soft Oṃ (or hum it silently) only as long as is comfortable, then rest in the quiet before the next easy in-breath. No forced hold and no straining to make the sound longer.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 39 does not prescribe a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Keep the sound soft and stop before the breath feels short. If you feel light-headed, return to ordinary breathing. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 016: praṇava fading into void. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 16, verse 39.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Any easy seat, or lie down. The map is one long sound tapering to silence.
  3. Orientation. Body relaxed, jaw and throat soft. Eyes closed or lowered.
  4. Breath. Easy in-breath. Let the out-breath carry a soft, long Om, aloud or silent, only as long as is comfortable. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. When the sound ends, stay right there in the quiet. Do not reach for the next thought.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: one sound thins into open silence. Metaphor only.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 39: sound the praṇava, and at the end of its prolonged sound contemplate emptiness; through that emptiness one reaches emptiness. Rest in the quiet gap. No straining.
  8. Exit / integrate. Breathe normally for a few rounds. Open the eyes.
