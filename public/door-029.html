The Fire of Time Rising Through One's Own Body

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 29 · verse 52 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 29Verse 52visualization/space · fire imageryHistorical / observe onlyDraft · awaiting guardian review
Historical / observe onlyThis dhāraṇā is kept as a classical description to read and understand. It is not practised here, and no practice cues are given. Observe-only. Imagery of one's own body burning can be distressing, especially for anyone with trauma or self-harm history; this school does not lead it. Read it as a description of letting limitation go. For practice, use a gentle space door such as Door 024 or Door 025.

Observe only: read and understand this classical description. It is not practised in class. For practice, use Door 024 or Door 025.

The verse

kālāgninā kālapadād utthitena svakam puram | pluṣṭam vicintayed ante śāntābhāsas tadā bhavet ||

Let one imagine one's own body (the 'city') burnt up by the fire of time (kālāgni) rising from the foot; at the end, one becomes a tranquil radiance.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: one utters a formula (aum ra-kṣa-ra-ya-uṃ tanuṃ dahayāmi namaḥ) and imagines one's body burnt by the flames of Kālāgni Rudra, rising from the toe of the right foot (kālapada); the image is of all the limitations of the body being burnt away, leaving one's essential nature, all peace. Wallis: 'one's own body (/fortress) is incinerated by the Fire of Time rising from the feet'.

Classical description

Classical method: while uttering a formula, one visualises the fire of time rising from the toe of the right foot and burning one's whole body, and at the end rests in a tranquil radiance.

Historical / observe only. Read and understood, not practised. No practice cues are given for this door.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Not led as a practice: visualising one's own body burning can be distressing. Anyone with trauma, self-harm history or strong anxiety should skip this imagery entirely. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
