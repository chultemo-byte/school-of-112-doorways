Door 082 · House 6 · Ājñā

Knowing and Willing in Everything, and Attention to the Link

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 82 · verses 105, 106 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 82Verses 105–106contemplationPracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

Recognise your own knowing and willing in everything around you, and stay aware of the link between knower and known.

The verse

ghaṭādau yac ca vijñānam icchādyaṃ vā mamāntare | naiva sarvagataṃ jātam bhāvayann iti sarvagaḥ || grāhyagrāhakasaṃvittiḥ sāmānyā sarvadehinām | yogināṃ tu viśeṣo 'sti sambandhe sāvadhānatā ||

(105) 'The knowing, willing and the rest that are in me are not in me alone; they arise in everything, in a pot and other things too': contemplating this, one becomes all-pervading. (106) The awareness of object and subject is common to all embodied beings; the yogīs' distinction is that they stay attentive to the relation between them.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: knowing (jñāna) and willing/acting (icchā, kriyā) are not the monopoly of human beings but common to everything; contemplating this, one sheds insularity and feels kinship with all. He quotes Abhinavagupta and Somānanda: 'I know as one with the jar'. Singh states that verse 106 contains no separate dhāraṇā; it reinforces 105 by naming the yogī's distinction: mindfulness of the witnessing awareness to which every object is related. Wallis counts the two verses as separate yuktis: 'consciousness in everything' (Y80) and 'pay attention to the connection' (Y81).

How to sit with it

Contemplate that the knowing and willing you feel in yourself are present in everything around you: a pot, a stone, a cloud. Then, as you look at any object, stay attentive to the link between you, the knower, and it, the known.

Where attention rests

Knowing and willing recognised as present in everything, not only in oneself

The relation between the one who knows and what is known

Not a body shape. The map is the same spark of knowing in oneself and in every object, and the thread that links knower to known.

Breath

Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps you settle, then let the count go. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 105–106 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. A contemplation, not a belief to be forced; let it remain light. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 082: knowing and willing in everything, and attention to the link. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 82, verses 105–106.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Sit with the eyes open or closed. The map is the same spark in you and in every object.
  3. Orientation. Let the floor or the chair carry you. Eyes closed or soft.
  4. Breath. Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. Feel your own knowing and willing. See the same in the pot, the stone, the cloud. Then stay aware of the link between you and what you see.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: one spark in every object. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 105–106: the knowing and willing in me are in all things, even a pot; contemplating so, one becomes all-pervading. The yogī stays attentive to the link between knower and known.
  8. Exit / integrate. Let the breath go unwatched for a round. Move the fingers and toes. Return to the room.
