Door 007 · House 1 · Mūlādhāra

Twelve Stations, Twelve Letters

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 7 · verse 30 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 7Verse 30visualization · sound/mantra · kuṇḍalinī / subtle bodyPracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

Move attention up the centre line one station at a time: feel it coarsely, then subtly, then let it go and rise.

The verse

kramadvādaśakaṃ samyag dvādaśākṣarabheditam | sthūlasūkṣmaparasthityā muktvā muktvāntataḥ śivaḥ ||

Twelve stations in sequence, each properly marked by one of twelve letters: attend to each in its gross, then subtle, then supreme form, leaving each behind in turn; in the end there is Śiva.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer; not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh's notes list the twelve stations (janmāgra at the level of the generative organ, mūla, kanda, nābhi, hṛd, kaṇṭha, tālu, bhrūmadhya, lalāṭa, brahmarandhra, śakti, vyāpinī) and pair them with twelve vowels (a ā i ī u ū e ai o au aṃ aḥ). The verse itself names neither the stations nor the letters: both lists are commentary.

How to sit with it

Classical method (Singh's notes): twelve stations from janmāgra to vyāpinī, linked with the twelve vowels, each contemplated in gross, subtle and supreme phases and then left behind. School simplification (flagged): move attention up a short midline sequence (pelvic floor, navel, heart, throat, brow, crown). At each station feel the coarse sense, then a subtler quiet, then leave it and rise. No phoneme placement, no forced breath-hold.

Where attention rests

One station at a time: feel it coarsely, then subtly, then let it go and rise

The release itself, each time a station is left

Not a body shape. The map is a ladder of twelve stations, each paired with a letter, climbed and released one at a time through gross, subtle and supreme phases.

Breath

Equal inhale four, exhale four if counting helps. Keep the breath quiet and even. No forced retention.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 30 does not prescribe a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. The school form is a simplification. It leaves out the vowel placements and the first commentarial station (janmāgra, at the level of the generative organ), and stops at the crown instead of continuing to the two stations beyond the body (śakti, vyāpinī). If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 007: twelve stations, twelve letters. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 7, verse 30.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Any easy seat, or lie down. The map is a ladder of stations up the centre of the body. We use a short six-step version.
  3. Orientation. Body supported. Eyes closed or lowered. Orient upward, one station at a time.
  4. Breath. Equal breath, four in, four out. No forced retention.
  5. Attention. Pelvic floor, navel, heart, throat, brow, crown: feel each coarsely, then subtly, then leave it and rise.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: piece-by-piece effort is released into continuity. Metaphor only.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 30: twelve stations, twelve letters, each held in gross, subtle and supreme form and then released; at the end, Śiva. Our form is simplified and safe: no drills on any genital station.
  8. Exit / integrate. Let the ladder go. Rest one breath at the top in open space. Open the eyes.
