Door 099 · House 7 · Sahasrāra

Bhairava Is Everywhere, Even in the Ordinary

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 99 · verse 124 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 99Verse 124contemplationPracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

Recognise the plain sense 'I am', and the ordinary world around you, as the sacred itself.

The verse

sarvatra bhairavo bhāvaḥ sāmānyeṣv api gocaraḥ | na ca tadvyatirekeṇa paro 'stīty advayā gatiḥ ||

The state of Bhairava is everywhere, within reach even of ordinary people; 'there is nothing other than That': one who knows this has the non-dual path.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: this dhāraṇā needs no particular meditation practice. Everyone, even the unlearned, knows the 'I'; the everyday 'I' is a symbol of the eternal 'I' throbbing in every heart, consciousness-bliss, which is Bhairava, known within; and He is known outwardly through His manifestation in the cosmos. He cites Maheśvarānanda's Mahārthamañjarī (verse 4) and calls this an example of anupāya, the 'means without means'. Wallis: 'the Bhairava state'.

How to sit with it

No technique is needed. Notice the plain sense 'I am' that everyone has, and the ordinary world around you. Recognise both as That, with nothing apart from it.

Where attention rests

The plain sense 'I am', present in everyone

The recognition that there is nothing apart from it

Not a body shape. The map is the plainest sense of 'I am', shared by everyone, and the whole world, with nothing outside them.

Breath

Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps you settle, then let the count go. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 124 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Nothing to achieve here; if it feels abstract, simply rest. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 099: Bhairava is everywhere, even in the ordinary. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 99, verse 124.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Sit, or carry this into the day. The map is the plain sense 'I am' and the ordinary world.
  3. Orientation. Sit comfortably, or carry this into ordinary activity. Eyes open or closed.
  4. Breath. Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. Notice the plain sense 'I am'. Notice the ordinary world. Nothing apart from That. Rest in it.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: the sacred in the ordinary. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 124: the state of Bhairava is everywhere, within reach even of ordinary people; knowing there is nothing apart from That, one is non-dual.
  8. Exit / integrate. Carry on with your day, noticing the sacred in the ordinary.
