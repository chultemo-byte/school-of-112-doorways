Door 011 · House 1 · Mūlādhāra

Mind Placed Inside the Skull

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 11 · verse 34 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 11Verse 34concentration · emptiness/spacePractice

Close the eyes and rest attention inside the skull as a soft field, letting steadiness build without force.

The verse

kapālāntar mano nyasya tiṣṭhan mīlitalocanaḥ | krameṇa manaso dārḍhyāl lakṣayel lakṣyam uttamam ||

Remaining with eyes closed, place the mind inside the skull; as steadiness of mind gradually grows, one perceives the highest thing to be perceived.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer; not a quotation.

Commentary

'tiṣṭhan' means remaining or abiding; Singh renders it 'seated', Bäumer 'sitting motionless', Silburn 'se tenant', Wallis 'remaining'. Satsangi locates the attention at the crown of the head and The Mystery Within glosses the place as the sahasrāra cakra; most translators say 'inside the cranium'. Singh also records an esoteric reading of kapāla as the union of Śiva and Śakti.

How to sit with it

Sit or lie quietly, close the eyes, and place attention inside the skull as a soft field rather than a clenched point. Let mental steadiness build bit by bit without forcing a vision or a blank.

Where attention rests

The steady space inside the skull, held as a soft field

Steadiness growing gradually, without forcing a vision or a blank

Not a body shape. The map is the bowl of space inside the skull, held as a soft field rather than a tight point. Closed eyes are part of the verse; posture is not specified.

Breath

Natural, even breath, about four in and four out. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 34 does not prescribe a body position (it specifies closed eyes and uses the verb tiṣṭhan, 'remaining', which Singh renders 'seated'). The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. If attention in the head brings pressure or a headache, move attention down to the breath at the belly or open the eyes and stop. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 011: mind placed inside the skull. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 11, verse 34.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Sit or lie in any steady way. The map is the bowl of space inside the head.
  3. Orientation. Body still and supported, head balanced. Close the eyes: the verse asks for that.
  4. Breath. Natural, even breath, four in and four out. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. Rest attention inside the skull as a soft field. Let steadiness grow by itself.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: scattered effort gathers into one steady field. Metaphor only.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 34: with eyes closed, place the mind inside the skull; as it steadies, one perceives the highest thing to be perceived. Do not force a vision or a blank.
  8. Exit / integrate. Let attention drop to the breath at the belly. Open the eyes slowly.
