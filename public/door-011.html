Water

Even counts. Inhale widen the side body; exhale root through both feet without collapsing the front knee.

Geometry

Polygon. Wide-stance T: base line (two feet), upright front shin pillar, horizontal arm line at shoulder height, torso column over pelvis facing the long side of the mat.

Feet wide; front foot points forward, back foot turned in about 45–80 degrees as the hips allow. Front heel roughly lines with back arch (or heel-to-heel if hips need more space). Front knee bends over the ankle without collapsing inward; back leg stays long and grounded. Arms reach to a T at shoulder height; torso stays upright and centered, not tipping toward the front leg.

Safety

  1. Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  2. General practice caution only; not medical advice. Shorten stance or reduce front-knee bend if knees, hips, or shoulders complain. Do not force the front thigh parallel or square the pelvis past what keeps the front knee tracking.
Start on the exhale.Soft knees.A chair still opens the door.If it hurts, stop.

Five ways

  1. This is Door D-011: Warrior II, Virabhadrasana II. Wide stance, arms like a T, gaze soft past the front hand.
  2. Feet wide. Front heel toward back arch. Bend the front knee over the ankle. Reach both arms to shoulder height. Keep the torso upright over the pelvis.
  3. Face the long side of the mat. Crown over pelvis. Front knee tracks mid-foot. Weight shares both feet. Soft gaze past the front fingertips.
  4. Breathe even fours: inhale four, exhale four, six cycles. Let the breath land in the side ribs. Do not hold the breath to force depth.
  5. Straighten the front leg, lower the arms, step the feet together. Stand a breath and feel the same soft relating without the shape.
