Door 094 · House 6 · Ājñā

Seeing a Familiar Place, Letting Memories Go

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 94 · verse 119 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 94Verse 119ordinary life · sight · memoryPracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

At a familiar place, let its memories go and see it fresh, with the body feeling open and unsupported.

The verse

vastuṣu smaryamāṇeṣu dṛṣṭe deśe manas tyajet | svaśarīraṃ nirādhāraṃ kṛtvā prasarati prabhuḥ ||

When a place is seen and things are being remembered, let one release the mind from them; making one's own body supportless, the Lord spreads forth.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: on remembering something, ignore the remembered object and fix the mind on the original experience on which the memory rests, while detaching mentally from the body-mind in which memories and impressions are stored; the mind then returns to pure experience, which is Bhairava's nature. He classes it as śāktopāya. Wallis: 'when seeing a place one has been before, let go of things that arise in the memory, and let your body be supportless'.

How to sit with it

When you see a place you know, notice the memories it brings up and let them go, one by one, without following them. See the place as it is right now. Feel the body as if it were resting on nothing, open. Rest in the plain experience of seeing.

Where attention rests

The place as it is seen now, without the memories it calls up

The body felt as supportless

Not a body shape. The map is a familiar place seen fresh, with its memories set down and the body felt as resting on nothing.

Breath

Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps you settle, then let the count go. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 119 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. If the place carries painful memories, do not force this; choose a neutral place, or come back to it with support. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 094: seeing a familiar place, letting memories go. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 94, verse 119.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Look at a familiar place, outdoors or in. The map is the place seen fresh, its memories set down.
  3. Orientation. Sit or stand where you can see a familiar place. Eyes open, soft.
  4. Breath. Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. Notice the memories this place brings. Let them go without following. See it as it is now. Feel the body resting on nothing.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: a familiar place seen fresh. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 119: when a place is seen and things are remembered, release the mind from them; making the body supportless, the Lord spreads forth.
  8. Exit / integrate. Let the breath go unwatched for a round. Move the fingers and toes. Return to the room.
