Door 100 · House 7 · Sahasrāra

Equal Toward Friend and Foe

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 100 · verse 125 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 100Verse 125ordinary life · contemplationPracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

Meet friend and foe, praise and blame, with the same even mind.

The verse

samaḥ śatrau ca mitre ca samo mānāvamānayoḥ | brahmaṇaḥ paripūrṇatvād iti jñātvā sukhī bhavet ||

Being the same toward enemy and friend, the same in honour and dishonour, knowing that brahman is full everywhere, one becomes happy.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: from the conviction that brahman is present everywhere comes equality of consciousness (samatā): the same goodwill toward all, neither elated by honour nor depressed by dishonour. He compares Bhagavad Gītā 5.18 and 14.25. Wallis: 'equal towards all'.

How to sit with it

Bring to mind someone you like and someone you find difficult; a moment of praise and a moment of blame. Recognise the same fullness present in each, and let the mind settle level, leaning toward neither. Carry that evenness into the day.

Where attention rests

The same attitude toward friend and foe, praise and blame

The fullness present in everyone

Not a body shape. The map is a level balance with friend and foe, praise and blame on either side, held even by the fullness present in both.

Breath

Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps you settle, then let the count go. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 125 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Equanimity does not mean accepting harm: keep your boundaries, and step away from people or situations that are unsafe. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 100: equal toward friend and foe. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 100, verse 125.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Sit, or carry this into the day. The map is a level balance: friend and foe, praise and blame.
  3. Orientation. Let the floor or the chair carry you. Eyes closed or soft.
  4. Breath. Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. Bring to mind a friend, then someone difficult. A moment of praise, then of blame. The same fullness in each. Stay level.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: a balance held level. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 125: the same toward enemy and friend, honour and dishonour, knowing brahman is full everywhere, one becomes happy.
  8. Exit / integrate. Take a slow, free breath. Carry the level mind into your next meeting with someone.
