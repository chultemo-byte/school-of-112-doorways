Retained Breath and the Tranquil

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 4 · verse 27 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

The verse

kumbhitā recitā vāpi pūritā vā yadā bhavet | tadante śāntanāmāsau śaktyā śāntaḥ prakāśate ||

When the breath-power is held (kumbhita), whether after it has been let out or after it has been drawn in, then at the end of that holding the power called 'the tranquil' (śāntā) reveals the Tranquil One.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer; not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: exhalation retained outside and inhalation retained inside; at the end of the practice the śakti is called śāntā. Wallis groups verses 24 to 27 as one breath technique, with this verse as its retention phase.

Classical description

Classical method: retention of the breath (kumbhaka) after exhaling or after inhaling, followed by resting in the tranquillity at the end of the retention.

Historical / observe only. Read and understood, not practised. No practice cues are given for this door.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Deliberate breath retention is not taught by this school. Breath-holding can bring dizziness, light-headedness or fainting and is not suitable for everyone, for example during pregnancy or for people with heart, blood-pressure or breathing conditions. Anyone who wants to learn retention should do so only with a qualified teacher and their own clinician's advice. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
