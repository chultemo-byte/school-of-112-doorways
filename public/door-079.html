Door 079 · House 5 · Viśuddha

The World as a Magic Show or a Painting

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 79 · verse 102 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 79Verse 102contemplationPracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

Look at the world around you as a vivid magic show or painting, and notice the lightness that comes.

The verse

indrajālamayaṃ viśvaṃ vyastaṃ vā citrakarmavat | bhramad vā dhyāyataḥ sarvam paśyataś ca sukhodgamaḥ ||

For one who contemplates the whole universe as a magic show, or as scattered like the figures of a painting, or as whirling, and who sees everything in this way, happiness arises.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: the cosmos is seen as jugglery conjured by a magician, as the configuration of a painting, or as illusory as the apparent movement of trees seen from a moving boat; Jayaratha cites this verse too as an example of sarvātma-saṃkoca. Wallis: a magic show, a wondrous painting, or constantly in flux.

How to sit with it

Look around, or recall the day, and see it as a magic show, a painting, or a scene passing from a moving boat: vivid, but not solid. Hold that view gently and notice the lightness and happiness that arise.

Where attention rests

The world seen as a magic show, a painting, or a scene in constant motion

The happiness that comes with that lightness

Not a body shape. The map is the world around you seen as a display, vivid but not solid, like a painting or a conjured scene.

Breath

Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps you settle, then let the count go. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 102 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Seeing the world as a display does not mean other people's pain is unreal; keep care and responsibility in view. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 079: the world as a magic show or a painting. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 79, verse 102.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Sit with the eyes open, or recall the day. The map is the world as a vivid display.
  3. Orientation. Sit comfortably. Eyes open, taking in the scene, or closed, recalling the day.
  4. Breath. Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. See what is around you as a magic show, a painting, a scene passing from a boat. Vivid, not solid. Notice the lightness.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: a heavy world becoming a vivid display. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 102: contemplating the universe as a magic show, a painting or a whirl, and seeing everything so, happiness arises.
  8. Exit / integrate. Drop the exercise. Notice the room: its sounds, its light. Carry the quiet into what comes next.
