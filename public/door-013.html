Water

Inhale lengthen the side body; exhale rotate a little more around that length. Never yank the neck or force the bottom hand lower.

Geometry

Polygon. triangle + spiral axis

From a wide standing base, the front foot points forward and the back foot turns in. Both legs stay long (soften the front knee if the hamstring shouts). The torso hinges over the front leg, then rotates toward the front thigh so the bottom hand meets a block, the shin, or the floor, and the top arm reaches up. The shape is a triangle in the legs with a long spiral through the spine.

Safety

  1. Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  2. Hand on a block or the shin instead of the floor; shorten the stance; keep gaze down if the neck complains.
  3. General practice caution for deep twists, hamstrings, and neck. Not medical advice. Skip classical sensory-seal pressure on the eyes in class.

Five ways

  1. This is Door 013: Revolved Triangle, Parivrtta Trikonasana. Standing base, long legs, a twist toward the front thigh.
  2. Step wide. Front foot forward, back foot turned in. Hinge over the front leg, then spiral the ribs. Bottom hand to block, shin, or floor; top arm reaches up.
  3. Feel a long line from back heel to crown. Gravity stays in both feet. Gaze up only if the neck is quiet; otherwise look down or forward.
  4. Inhale for four counts and lengthen. Exhale for six and rotate a little around that length. Six easy cycles. No yanking.
  5. Inhale unwind the ribs to center. Exhale hand to hip and rise. Step the feet together. Stand for one breath and notice the after-feel of the spiral.
