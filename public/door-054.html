The Five Seals at the Moment of Seeing

The verse

karaṅkiṇyā krodhanayā bhairavyā lelihānayā | khecaryā dṛṣṭikāle ca parāvāptiḥ prakāśate ||

Through the seals (mudrā) called karaṅkiṇī, krodhanā, bhairavī, lelihānā and khecarī, at the moment of seeing, the supreme attainment shines forth.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh, following Lakshmanjoo, takes dṛṣṭikāla as 'at the time of practice' and describes each mudrā: karaṅkiṇī views the world as a mere skeleton and the body as dissolved in the highest space; krodhanā is a tense, tight disposition; bhairavī keeps the eyes fixed outward without blinking while attention turns inward; lelihānā 'tastes' the universe as one's own I-consciousness; khecarī has several forms, including turning the tongue back toward the palate, and in its highest form is remaining in Śiva-consciousness. Wallis notes that the Sanskrit commentaries are needed to understand the verse and counts the five mudrās as five yuktis (Y49–53).

Classical description

Classical method: five seals (karaṅkiṇī, krodhanā, bhairavī, lelihānā, khecarī), each described only in commentary, are practised at the moment of perception, and the supreme attainment shines forth.

Historical / observe only. Read and understood, not practised. No practice cues are given for this door.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. The physical forms of these seals (tongue-turning, sustained unblinking gaze, tense holding) are not taught by this school. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
