Door 058 · House 4 · Anāhata

Mouth Open, Mentally Sounding 'Ha'

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 58 · verse 81 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 58Verse 81sound · mantra (mental)PracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

Let the mouth fall open, rest the tongue in the middle, and silently sound 'ha'.

The verse

madhyajihve sphāritāsye madhye nikṣipya cetanām | hoccāraṃ manasā kurvaṃs tataḥ śānte pralīyate ||

With the mouth wide open and the tongue in the middle, placing awareness in the middle, and sounding 'ha' in the mind, one dissolves into peace.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh reads the tongue as inverted against the palate (khecarī mudrā, as described in the Vivekamārtaṇḍa) and explains: the breath naturally sounds haṃsa; with the tongue inverted the 's' cannot form and only 'ha' remains, a vowel-less 'ha' that can only be sounded mentally, and repeating it develops the middle state. Wallis and Bäumer read 'the tongue in the middle of the wide open mouth'; Wallis adds 'pointing up'. This door follows the plain reading and does not teach tongue inversion.

How to sit with it

Let the jaw relax so the mouth opens wide, without strain. Rest the tongue in the middle of the mouth. Place awareness in the middle of that open space and sound 'ha' silently, in the mind only. Let it dissolve into peace.

Where attention rests

The middle of the open mouth

The silent, mental 'ha'

Not a body shape. The map is the open space of the mouth, the tongue resting in its middle, and a silent 'ha' sounding in that space.

Breath

Natural breath through the nose or the open mouth, whichever is comfortable. The 'ha' is silent, in the mind only. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 81 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Open the mouth only as wide as is comfortable; close it whenever the jaw tires. Tongue inversion is not taught. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 058: mouth open, mentally sounding 'ha'. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 58, verse 81.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Sit comfortably. The map is the open space of the mouth with the tongue resting in its middle.
  3. Orientation. Let the floor or the chair carry you. Eyes closed or soft.
  4. Breath. Natural breath, nose or mouth, whichever is easy. The 'ha' is silent. No hold.
  5. Attention. Let the jaw drop. Rest the tongue in the middle. Place awareness there and sound 'ha' silently, in the mind. Let it dissolve.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: a held jaw letting go into peace. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 81: mouth wide open, tongue in the middle, awareness in the middle, sounding 'ha' in the mind, one dissolves into peace.
  8. Exit / integrate. Let the breath go unwatched for a round. Move the fingers and toes. Return to the room.
