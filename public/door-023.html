A Moment of Void in the Body

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 23 · verse 46 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 23Verse 46visualization/space · sensationPracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

For a moment, sense one part of the body, such as a hand, as empty space.

The verse

tanūdeśe śūnyataiva kṣaṇamātraṃ vibhāvayet | nirvikalpaṃ nirvikalpo nirvikalpasvarūpabhāk ||

If, even for a single moment, one contemplates some region of the body as nothing but void, free of thought-constructs, one becomes thought-free and takes on the nature of the thought-free.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh reads tanūdeśa as the body taken as the limited empirical self and describes three stages: contemplating it without thought-constructs, becoming habitually thought-free, and finally entering the state of Bhairava, whose nature is thought-free. Wallis and Bäumer read tanūdeśa as 'any part (or point) of the body'. This door follows the 'any part of the body' reading and notes Singh's.

How to sit with it

Choose one region of the body, such as a hand. For a moment, sense it as nothing but open space, without thinking about it. Let the moment end naturally; repeat a few times.

Where attention rests

One region of the body felt as void, just for a moment

The thought-free quality of that moment

Not a body shape. The map is one small region of the body felt, just for a moment, as empty space.

Breath

Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps you settle, then let the count go. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 46 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Keep it brief and light; if numbness or unease appears, move the hand and feel it normally. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 023: a moment of void in the body. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 23, verse 46.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Sit or lie down. The map is one small region, such as a hand, felt as open space.
  3. Orientation. Let the floor or the chair carry you. Eyes closed or soft.
  4. Breath. Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. Choose a hand. For one moment, sense it as empty space, without thinking about it. Let the moment go. Repeat.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: something solid becoming open for a moment. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 46: contemplate a region of the body as pure void even for a moment, without thoughts, and one becomes thought-free.
  8. Exit / integrate. Drop the exercise. Notice the room: its sounds, its light. Carry the quiet into what comes next.
