Not Turning Back from the Two Spaces

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 2 · verse 25 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 2Verse 25breath · gap/pausePractice

Breathe naturally and stay present in the small space after each in-breath and each out-breath, without hurrying on.

The verse

maruto 'ntar bahir vāpi viyadyugmānivartanāt | bhairavyā bhairavasyetthaṃ bhairavi vyajyate vapuḥ ||

O Bhairavī, when the breath, whether it has moved inward or outward, is not hurried back from the two spaces where it comes to rest, the inner and the outer, then in this way Bhairava's form shows itself through Bhairavī.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer; not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh describes the two spaces as the split-second pauses at the heart-centre and at the outer dvādaśānta. Singh calls the two pauses the outer and inner kumbhaka but describes them as split-second natural pauses that are only observed; this school follows that observational reading.

How to sit with it

Breathe naturally. Notice the small space after the in-breath and the small space after the out-breath. Stay present in each space instead of hurrying attention back into the next movement. Do not hold the breath to make the space longer.

Where attention rests

The two spaces where the breath rests, inside and outside

The habit of rushing into the next breath, noticed and softened

Not a body shape. The verse names a pair of spaces (viyad-yugma), one inside and one outside, where the breath pauses. The practice is to stay in each space a moment longer before attention rushes on.

Breath

Natural breath. Let each pause last exactly as long as it naturally lasts; do not lengthen it on purpose. Never force the pause.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 25 does not prescribe a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Do not convert this into breath-holding. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 002: not turning back from the two spaces. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 2, verse 25.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Any easy seat, or lie down. The map is a pair of spaces: one inside after the in-breath, one outside after the out-breath.
  3. Orientation. Let the body be fully held by the floor or chair. Eyes closed or lowered. Face the two spaces.
  4. Breath. Natural breath, about four in and four out if you like to count. Never force the pause.
  5. Attention. Stay present in the inner space and the outer space. Notice the urge to rush on, and let it soften.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: a hurried cycle opens into two small spaces. Metaphor only.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 25: by not turning back from the two spaces, inner and outer, Bhairava's form shows itself through Bhairavī. Do not hold the breath; just do not hurry away.
  8. Exit / integrate. Let the breath go unwatched for one round. Open the eyes slowly.
