Whirling Until Falling (Classical)

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 87 · verse 111 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 87Verse 111sensation · stillness after movementHistorical / observe onlyDraft · awaiting guardian review
Historical / observe onlyThis dhāraṇā is kept as a classical description to read and understand. It is not practised here, and no practice cues are given. Observe-only. Spinning until you fall can cause falls, head and joint injuries, nausea and fainting, and is unsafe with heart, blood-pressure, inner-ear or balance conditions and in pregnancy. For a gentle practice of stillness after movement, use Door 060.

Observe only: read and understand this classical description. It is not practised in class. Never spin until falling. For a gentle practice of stillness after movement, use Door 060.

The verse

bhrāntvā bhrāntvā śarīreṇa tvaritam bhuvi pātanāt | kṣobhaśaktivirāmeṇa parā saṃjāyate daśā ||

Whirling the body round and round, then swiftly falling to the ground: through the cessation of the energy of agitation, the supreme state arises.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh comments on verses 111 and 112 together: both refer to the condition of mind at the end of an intense commotion, here physical, in 112 intellectual. When the stirring ceases, thought-constructs are laid to rest and Bhairava's nature is revealed. He classes 111 as śāmbhavopāya. Wallis: 'cessation of the energy of excitation'.

Classical description

Classical method: whirling the body round and round and then swiftly falling to the ground, the supreme state arises through the cessation of the energy of agitation.

Historical / observe only. Read and understood, not practised. No practice cues are given for this door.

A door to practise instead

Door 060 · Gentle Rocking or Swaying

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Do not whirl or spin to the point of dizziness or falling. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
