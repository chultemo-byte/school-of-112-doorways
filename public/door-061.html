Gazing into a Clear Sky

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 61 · verse 84 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 61Verse 84sight · emptiness/spacePracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

Rest a steady gaze in a clear sky, away from the sun, and become still.

The verse

ākāśaṃ vimalam paśyan kṛtvā dṛṣṭiṃ nirantarām | stabdhātmā tatkṣaṇād devi bhairavaṃ vapur āpnuyāt ||

Looking at the clear sky with an uninterrupted gaze, becoming utterly still, in that very instant, O Goddess, one attains the form of Bhairava.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: becoming still means the sense of the body vanishes and thoughts and emotions are arrested; the vastness of the sky lets the viewer be lost in a sense of infinity; 'fixed eyes' means without blinking. Singh classes it as śāmbhavopāya. Wallis: 'sky-gazing'.

How to sit with it

Lie or sit where you can see a clear sky, well away from the sun. Let the gaze rest in it steadily and become still. Let the vastness take attention. Blink whenever needed.

Where attention rests

A clear, cloudless sky, seen without interruption

The stillness that comes with it

Not a body shape. The map is a clear sky filling the field of view, with nothing for the eye to hold on to.

Breath

Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps you settle, then let the count go. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position with a clear view of open sky; lying on the back outdoors works well.

School addition. Verse 84 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Never look at or near the sun. Blink whenever needed: the classical gaze is unblinking, but this school does not ask for it. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 061: gazing into a clear sky. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 61, verse 84.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. Sit or lie where you can see clear sky. The map is open sky filling the view.
  3. Orientation. Sit or lie comfortably. Eyes open and soft on clear sky. Never on the sun.
  4. Breath. Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. Let the gaze rest steadily in the sky, well away from the sun. Become still. Let the vastness take you. Blink when you need to.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: a bounded self dissolving into sky. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 84: looking at the clear sky with an unbroken gaze, utterly still, in that instant one attains the form of Bhairava.
  8. Exit / integrate. Release the attention. Feel the weight of the body on its support. Open the eyes when ready.
