Door 014 · House 1 · Mūlādhāra

The Point of Subtle Fire

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 14 · verse 37 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Historical / observe onlyThis dhāraṇā is kept as a classical description to read and understand. It is not practised here, and no practice cues are given. Never press or rub the eyes to produce light. This school does not teach eye pressure in any form.

Observe only: read the verse and its classical description. This door is not practised.

The verse

dhāmāntaḥkṣobhasambhūtasūkṣmāgnitilakākṛtim | binduṃ śikhānte hṛdaye layānte dhyāyato layaḥ ||

For one who meditates, at the top of the head or in the heart, on the bindu, a tilaka-like spark of subtle fire produced by agitation within the 'abode', there is dissolution at the end of dissolution.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer; not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh glosses the 'abode' (dhāma) as the light in the eyes and the agitation as pressure on the eyes. Bäumer has 'by agitating the eye', Chaudhri 'press the eyes gently' and Dubois 'une pression (exercée sur les yeux)' (all as printed in The Mystery Within). Singh also gives an alternative: the sparks seen as a lamp goes out. Wallis renders it more neutrally as 'inner stimulation of the Radiant Abode'.

Classical description

Classical method: produce a spark of light by pressure or agitation of the eyes, then meditate on it at the top of the head or in the heart until it dissolves.

Historical / observe only. Read and understood, not practised. No practice cues are given for this door.

A door to practise instead

Door 010 · Attention Dissolving into Space, a Wall, or a Worthy Vessel

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Never press or rub the eyes to produce light. This school does not teach eye pressure in any form. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
