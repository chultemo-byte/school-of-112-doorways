Soft toward the top hand or forward along the cheek if the neck complains.

Water

Inhale lengthen the top arm and outer side; exhale settle weight into the back heel and front foot without collapsing the chest.

Geometry

Polygon. long lateral diagonal (back heel → front knee → side ribs → top hand) over a wide base triangle

From a wide Warrior II stance, bend the front knee over the front ankle, tip the torso over the front thigh, and place the bottom hand on the floor, a block, or the front shin. Reach the top arm overhead into one long side-body line from back heel through outer ribs to fingertips. Keep the chest open toward the side/ceiling; never collapse the front knee inward.

While holding the shape (or seated after), sense the whole body at once and let attention spread evenly through front, back, sides, above, and below. Soften the idea of a hard body-edge; rest as if inner and outer space were the same field. Do not force a visualization or suppress thoughts.

Safety

  1. Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  2. Hands-on-hips side bend in a shorter stance, or Warrior II with soft side stretch; use a block under the bottom hand rather than reaching the floor.
  3. General practice caution only; not medical advice. Ease off if front knee, hip, or neck complains; shorten stance and raise the bottom hand.

Five ways

  1. Door D-014: Extended Side Angle — Utthita Parsvakonasana. One long side-body line over a wide base.
  2. Wide stance. Bend the front knee over the ankle. Tip the torso over the front thigh. Bottom hand to floor, block, or shin. Top arm reaches long overhead.
  3. Back heel heavy. Front knee tracks over the second toe. Bottom hand props lightly. Chest opens to the side, not folded down.
  4. Inhale four counts to lengthen the top side. Exhale six counts into both feet. Six cycles. Soften if the breath shortens.
  5. Inhale to lift the torso. Straighten the front leg. Step back to standing. Notice residual space-sense before the next shape.
