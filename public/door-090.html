Gazing into a Deep Well (Classical)

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 90 · verse 115 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 90Verse 115sight · height · spaceHistorical / observe onlyDraft · awaiting guardian review
Historical / observe onlyThis dhāraṇā is kept as a classical description to read and understand. It is not practised here, and no practice cues are given. Observe-only. Standing over a drop and gazing down deliberately provokes vertigo and fear and risks a fall. For a safe practice of gazing into open space, use Door 036 (the space inside a pot) or Door 061 (a clear sky).

Observe only: read and understand this classical description. It is not practised in class. Never stand over a drop to gaze down. For safe gazing practices, use Door 036 or Door 061.

The verse

kūpādike mahāgarte sthitvopari nirīkṣaṇāt | avikalpamateḥ samyak sadyaś cittalayaḥ sphuṭam ||

Standing above a well or some great chasm and gazing down into it, for one whose understanding is free of thought-constructs, the dissolution of the mind clearly happens at once.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh adds 'without blinking' in brackets: if the yogī gazes for a long time into the space of a deep well, or from a high summit, he feels giddiness and fear, and the inner pulse (spanda) throws him out of ordinary consciousness; if his intuitive understanding is pure, it is freed of thought-constructs and he knows surpassing peace. He classes it as śāmbhavopāya. Wallis: 'gazing into a well' (deep well, chasm or the like).

Classical description

Classical method: standing above a deep well or chasm and gazing down into it, the mind of one free of thought-constructs dissolves at once.

Historical / observe only. Read and understood, not practised. No practice cues are given for this door.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Do not stand at edges, wells, cliffs or heights to practise. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
