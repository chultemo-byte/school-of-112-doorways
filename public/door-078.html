Door 078 · House 5 · Viśuddha

Stillness at the Heart of a Strong Emotion

Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra · dhāraṇā 78 · verse 101 (Jaideva Singh numbering)

Door 78Verse 101ordinary life · sensation · emotionPracticeDraft · awaiting guardian review

In a strong emotion, set aside its story and rest, still and soft, inside the feeling itself.

The verse

kāmakrodhalobhamohamadamātsaryagocare | buddhiṃ nistimitāṃ kṛtvā tat tattvam avaśiṣyate ||

In the grip of desire, anger, greed, confusion, intoxication or envy, let one make the mind utterly still; then the reality underlying them alone remains.

School paraphrase drawing on Jaideva Singh (1979), Christopher Wallis and Bettina Bäumer (as printed in Wallis's concordance); not a quotation.

Commentary

Singh: under a strong emotion, dissociate the mind from its object and concentrate on the emotion itself, neither accepting nor rejecting it, withdrawing inward like a tortoise into its shell; the passion is calmed 'like a charmed snake', and one touches the inner pulse (spanda). He calls this ātma-saṃkoca or śakti-saṃkoca. Wallis: make the mind still and soft right in the middle of the emotion; reality is what remains.

How to sit with it

When a strong emotion arises (anger, craving, envy), set aside its object and story. Turn toward the feeling itself and let the mind become very still and soft inside it, neither acting on it nor pushing it away. Stay until only its bare energy, or quiet, remains.

Where attention rests

The emotion itself, felt directly, with the mind held still and soft

What remains when the emotion has passed through

Not a body shape. The map is a storm with a still centre: the emotion swirling, and attention resting motionless in its middle.

Breath

Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps you settle, then let the count go. No forced hold.

The counts are an optional school pacing aid, not part of the verse. Hold is always 0: this school never adds a deliberate breath-hold.

Any steady, comfortable position (school addition)

Any steady, comfortable position: sitting on a chair, sitting on the floor with support under the hips, or lying on the back. Standing or walking slowly is also acceptable when that is easier. Choose the position in which the body needs the least effort.

School addition. Verse 101 does not specify a body position. The seat is a convenience so the dharana can be practised; it is not part of the technique and makes no claim of tradition.

Safety

  • Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  • General practice caution only; not medical advice. Never suppress or bottle feelings, and do not use this in place of getting help. If an emotion is overwhelming or brings thoughts of harming yourself or others, step away, ground yourself and reach out to someone you trust or a local support service. If anything feels distressing (dizziness, agitation, strong emotion), stop, open the eyes and rest attention on the feet and the floor.
  • This door needs no particular posture: sit on a chair, sit on the floor with support, or lie down. Eyes may stay open.

The eight beats (spoken script)
  1. Name the door. This is Door 078: stillness at the heart of a strong emotion. Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra dharana 78, verse 101.
  2. Geometry. No shape to make. This one is used when strong feelings arise. The map is a storm with a still centre.
  3. Orientation. Wherever you are, pause. Eyes closed or open. Turn toward the feeling, not its cause.
  4. Breath. Natural breath. Count about four in and four out only if it helps. No forced hold.
  5. Attention. Set aside the story and the object. Feel the emotion itself. Let the mind become still and soft inside it. Stay until only its energy remains.
  6. Reaction cue. School image: a still centre in the storm. A metaphor, not medicine.
  7. Adiyogi thread. Verse 101: in the grip of desire, anger, greed, confusion, intoxication or envy, make the mind utterly still; the reality beneath them alone remains.
  8. Exit / integrate. Take a slow, free breath. Feel the feet on the ground. Then respond to the situation from the quiet, if a response is needed.
