Water

Lower on an exhale when entering. In the hold, breathe short equal counts (about 3 in / 3 out) for up to four easy cycles. If breath gets hard or jagged, regress to knees or exit to plank or child's pose.

Geometry

Polygon. long staff rectangle on four contact points (two hands, two feet)

From a high plank line, the body lowers as one staff until the upper arms are roughly parallel to the floor and the elbows track close beside the ribs. Hands stay under the shoulders (or slightly forward if that keeps the shoulders stable). Heels press back, legs firm, hips level with the shoulders so the torso does not sag or pike. The polygon is a long low rectangle: four limbs as posts, torso and legs as one continuous beam.

Safety

  1. Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain.
  2. Knees-down Four-Limbed Staff (knees on the floor, same elbow-rib geometry) or stay in high plank / forearm plank if wrists or shoulders complain. Child's pose is always a valid exit.
  3. General practice caution only; not medical advice. Ease or skip if wrists, elbows, shoulders, or low back complain; do not force the shoulders below the elbows or collapse the midsection.

Five ways

  1. This is Door D-008, Four-Limbed Staff, Chaturanga Dandasana. A low, held staff on hands and feet.
  2. From plank, lower as one beam until upper arms are about parallel to the floor. Elbows hug the ribs. Hips level with shoulders. Heels press back.
  3. One line from heels through crown. Shoulders stay above the elbows. Soft gaze forward of the hands. Neck long.
  4. Enter on an exhale. In the hold, breathe about three in and three out for up to four easy cycles. Jagged breath means regress or exit.
  5. Press up to plank, drop knees, or move to child's pose. Optional: close the eyes for two breaths and rest attention inside the skull. Never force the next shape.
