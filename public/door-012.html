Soft toward the top hand if the neck is free; otherwise keep the gaze level with the side of the room.

Water

Inhale to lengthen the underside; exhale to settle weight into both feet without dumping into the front hip. Equal four-count breath for six cycles, then switch sides.

Geometry

Polygon. wide-base triangle (two feet + floor) plus long side diagonal (front ankle to hip to top hand)

Stand in a wide stance with the front foot roughly forward and the back foot angled. Keep both legs long and fold the torso sideways over the front leg so the lower hand finds shin, ankle, block, or floor without collapsing the underside waist. The top arm reaches on the same diagonal. The legs and floor make one triangle; torso and arms make a long slanted line.

Safety

  1. Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain in hamstring, knee, sacroiliac joint, or neck.
  2. Shorten the stance, rest the lower hand on a block or shin, and keep the gaze neutral if balance, neck, or hamstrings complain. General practice caution only; not medical advice.
Start on the exhale.Soft knees.A chair still opens the door.If it hurts, stop.

Five ways

  1. This is Door Twelve: Triangle, Utthita Trikonasana. A wide stance that opens one long side of the body.
  2. Feet wide. Front toes forward, back foot angled. Fold sideways over the front leg. Lower hand to shin or block; top arm reaches on the same diagonal.
  3. Root both feet. Lift the underside waist so ribs do not dump onto the thigh. Gaze to the top hand only if the neck stays easy.
  4. Breathe four in, four out. Let the inhale lengthen the open side; let the exhale settle the feet. Six cycles, then change sides.
  5. Inhale to rise with a long spine. Step the feet together. Stand quiet and feel both sides even before you leave.
