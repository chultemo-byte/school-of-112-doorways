Geometry

Polygon. asymmetric_lunge_trapezoid

From kneeling, step one foot forward so the front knee stacks over the ankle and the back knee rests on the floor. Hips square toward the front edge of the mat as much as honesty allows. Torso rises as a vertical column; hands may rest on the front thigh, frame the front foot, or reach overhead. The shape is a long grounded base with a lifted centerline—not a deepest-backbend contest.

Safety

  1. Pain is information. Pause, regress, or exit. Never push through sharp pain in the front knee, back knee, hips, or low back.
  2. If kneeling is unavailable: take a high lunge with the back knee lifted and hands on blocks, or practice the rising-attention thread seated upright in an open seat.
  3. General practice caution only; not medical advice. Pad the back knee. Keep the front knee tracking over the ankle, not collapsing inward. Skip deep backbending versions if the low back complains.

Five ways

  1. This is Door 005, Low Lunge — Anjaneyasana. One foot forward, back knee down, centerline rising. A doorway of base and lift, not a pose to win.
  2. Front knee stacks over the ankle. Back knee rests on the mat. Hips face forward as honestly as they can. Torso stands tall; arms may rise. Keep the shape long and steady.
  3. Feel gravity in the back knee and front foot. Level the pelvis. Stack the spine over the hips. Gaze soft forward. Do not force the low back into a deep arch.
  4. Inhale for four. Exhale for four. Six easy cycles. Let the breath land low in the pelvis, then rise softly through the ribs without gripping.
  5. Hands frame the front foot. Step back to a stable kneel or your next shape. Stand or sit. Notice the after-feel of base and rise. Pain means pause.
