# 004 · The Sri Yantra Carpet

*Archive entry · a visible yantra, read many ways*

---

## What it is

A carpet woven as the **Sri Yantra** — nine interlocking triangles, four pointing up, five pointing down, nested inside two rings of lotus petals and a square of gateways, all gathered around a single point: the **bindu**. Forty-three smaller triangles live inside the nine. It is the visual body of the goddess Tripura Sundari, the Beauty of the Three Worlds, and the map of how the one consciousness unfolds into the many and folds back again.

The tradition calls it a *yantra* — a machine. Not a picture to admire, but an instrument to sit with.

---

## Why this geometry, for meditation

Every triangle contains smaller triangles; every ring contains the whole. The eye never hits a wall — attention keeps finding new detail, and that sustained, effortless focus is exactly *dharana*. The yantra is a staircase the gaze walks down, not a wall it leans on.

The proportions carry the **golden ratio** and nested equilateral triangles — the same harmonic family as a flower or a nautilus shell. Sit with it long enough and the breathing slows to match. Not because the carpet emits a tone, but because the visual rhythm entrains the nervous system. That is the mechanism, and it is enough.

The deeper claim: the mantra and the diagram are the same thing in two forms — sound and shape. The "vibration" is not a number of hertz. It is the repetition of a single pattern until the pattern stops being something you see and becomes something you are. That is what staying in the place between thoughts feels like when the geometry is doing the holding.

---

## The variants — one carpet, four doors

The same nine triangles can be read as:

- **cosmology** — creation unfolding from the bindu outward, returning inward
- **the chakras stacked** — the nine *avarana* as layers of the subtle body
- **the body of the goddess** — Tripura Sundari herself, not a portrait of her
- **a map of the breath** — inhale as expansion, exhale as return to the point

A visitor who comes for the art leaves with a practice. A meditator who comes for the practice finds the art. Every depth of attention is rewarded equally — that is why it earns the center of the Archive.

---

## The mantra

**Om Aim Hrim Shrim Tripurasundaryai Namah**

"Salutations to the Beauty of the Three Worlds." Three seed sounds — *Aim* for speech and knowing, *Hrim* for illumination and the heart, *Shrim* for abundance and grace — gathered into one salutation. The full fifteen-syllable Panchadashi lives behind it, given only after initiation; this is the public skeleton, and it is enough to sit with.

---

## How to sit with this carpet

1. Place the carpet where the bindu is at eye level or just below — a low seat, a cushion on the floor.
2. Soften the gaze onto the **bindu**, the single point at the center. Not a stare — a resting.
3. Chant the mantra **one hundred times**, slowly, one repetition per breath if you can. The sound and the geometry do the same job from opposite directions: the ear holds the sound, the eye holds the shape, and both point inward.
4. When the count finishes — or before it does — let the mantra go. Stay in the quiet between the last sound and the next thought. That is the door.
5. If the mind wanders, return to the bindu. The geometry catches you; you do not have to catch yourself.

Pain is information. Drowsiness is information. Either means soften, open the eyes, or stand. The carpet does not demand — it invites, and it waits.

---

## What can happen

Nothing is promised, and nothing is sold. What the tradition and the geometry together suggest: the fractal keeps attention from collapsing, the breath slows on its own, and the space between thoughts — the *shanta* the Vijnana Bhairava points to — becomes briefly visible, like the bindu itself. Sometimes the carpet disappears and only the point remains. Sometimes nothing happens at all, and that is also the practice.
